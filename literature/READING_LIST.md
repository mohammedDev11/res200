# Paper-First Reading List: Audio and Spoken Question Answering

Last checked: 2026-08-26

This is a curated starting map, not a claim that the literature search is
complete. Links point to original publisher, proceedings, preprint, or official
project pages. Read the papers themselves and record evidence in your own notes.

## Understand the task boundary first

The name “question answering from audio” covers several different experimental
setups. Before comparing papers, identify:

1. Is the **evidence** environmental audio, speech, or both?
2. Is the **question** text or speech?
3. Is the **answer** a class, text span, free text, or speech?
4. Is the system a cascade (ASR then text QA) or end-to-end audio-to-answer?
5. Are recordings natural, crowd-recorded, or synthesized with TTS?
6. Are questions and answers human-authored, transformed from another dataset,
   or generated by a model?

Results across different setups are not directly comparable.

## Stage 1 — Foundations

### 1. Spoken SQuAD: A Study of Mitigating the Impact of Speech Recognition Errors on Listening Comprehension

- Lee, Wu, Liu, and Lee (Interspeech 2018)
- [Official dataset/project page](https://github.com/Chia-Hsuan-Lee/Spoken-SQuAD)
- [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.00320)
- Why read it: an early large-scale formulation of spoken-document QA and a
  clear introduction to the effect of ASR errors on extractive QA.
- Provenance warning: the spoken documents were synthesized from SQuAD with
  text-to-speech; the questions remain text. It is valuable, but it is not a
  naturally recorded spoken-QA corpus.

### 2. SpeechBERT: An Audio-and-text Jointly Learned Language Model for End-to-end Spoken Question Answering

- Chuang, Liu, Lee, and Lee (Interspeech 2020)
- [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.11559)
- Why read it: contrasts cascaded ASR→QA with joint audio-text representation
  learning and motivates end-to-end spoken QA.

## Stage 2 — Human-annotated audio QA

### 3. Clotho-AQA: A Crowdsourced Dataset for Audio Question Answering

- Lipping, Sudarsanam, Drossos, and Virtanen (EUSIPCO 2022)
- [Proceedings paper](https://eurasip.org/Proceedings/Eusipco/Eusipco2022/pdfs/0001140.pdf)
- [Dataset record](https://zenodo.org/records/6473207)
- Why read it: establishes a focused AQA benchmark for environmental audio and
  documents its crowdsourced question/answer collection and baseline models.
- Data facts to verify in the paper: 1,991 Clotho audio files; six questions per
  file; yes/no and single-word answer settings; three answers per question.
- Limitation to examine: restricted answer formats and possible linguistic or
  label biases can reward classification more than open reasoning.

### 4. Attention-Based Methods for Audio Question Answering

- Sudarsanam and Virtanen (2023)
- [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19769)
- Why read it: a direct methodological follow-up to Clotho-AQA using self- and
  cross-attention; it also analyzes answer normalization and dataset issues.

## Stage 3 — Real speech variation, dialogue, and fairness

### 5. SD-QA: Spoken Dialectal Question Answering for the Real World

- Faisal, Keshava, Alam, and Anastasopoulos (Findings of EMNLP 2021)
- [ACL Anthology page and PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2021.findings-emnlp.281/)
- Why read it: introduces a multidialect spoken QA benchmark spanning Arabic,
  Bengali, English, Kiswahili, and Korean, and studies ASR/QA fairness.
- Data facts to verify: more than 68,000 audio prompts, 24 dialects, and 255
  speakers.
- Especially relevant to RES200: Arabic dialect variation and the risk that
  aggregate scores hide population-level performance gaps.

### 6. End-to-end Spoken Conversational Question Answering: Task, Dataset and Model

- You et al. (Findings of NAACL 2022)
- [ACL Anthology page and PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2022.findings-naacl.91/)
- Why read it: extends spoken QA to multi-turn conversational questions and
  introduces Spoken-CoQA and cross-modal data distillation.
- Data facts to verify: more than 40,000 QA pairs from roughly 4,000
  conversations.

### 7. A Framework for Automatic Generation of Spoken Question-Answering Data

- Ünlü Menevşe, Manav, Arisoy, and Özgür (Findings of EMNLP 2022)
- [ACL Anthology page and PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2022.findings-emnlp.342/)
- Why read it: shows how QG, TTS, and ASR can create low-resource SQA data and
  introduces TurQuAse for Turkish.
- Provenance warning: this paper is essential for understanding synthetic data,
  but its generated questions and speech should not be confused with naturally
  collected data.

### 8. Dealing with Data Scarcity in Spoken Question Answering

- Ünlü Menevşe, Manav, Arisoy, and Özgür (LREC-COLING 2024)
- [ACL Anthology page and PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.397/)
- Why read it: studies limited annotation, automatic QA generation, and
  fine-tuning strategy. Use it to learn how to report gains without hiding the
  origin of generated training data.

## Stage 4 — Modern audio-language evaluation

### 9. AudioBench: A Universal Benchmark for Audio Large Language Models

- Wang et al. (NAACL 2025)
- [ACL Anthology page and PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.218/)
- Why read it: organizes speech, audio-scene, voice, and instruction tasks in a
  broader evaluation framework; includes SQA and AQA subsets.
- Provenance task: inspect each component separately because the benchmark mixes
  existing data, synthesized instructions, machine-generated candidates, and
  human verification.

### 10. VoxEval: Benchmarking the Knowledge Understanding Capabilities of End-to-End Spoken Language Models

- Cui, Jiao, Meng, and King (ACL 2025)
- [ACL Anthology page and PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.818/)
- [Official code and data](https://github.com/dreamtheater123/VoxEval)
- Why read it: evaluates speech-in/speech-out knowledge QA, robustness to audio
  conditions, and spoken mathematical reasoning in end-to-end speech models.

### 11. SpokenNativQA: Multilingual Everyday Spoken Queries for LLMs

- Alam, Hasan, and Chowdhury (Interspeech 2025)
- [ISCA Archive paper page](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2025/alam25_interspeech.html)
- Why read it: emphasizes naturally spoken, culturally aligned, multilingual
  questions and answers rather than only TTS conversions of text benchmarks.
- Data fact to verify: approximately 33,000 spoken QA items.

## Optional adjacent reading

These are useful when the research question expands beyond direct QA:

- [CoQA: A Conversational Question Answering Challenge](https://aclanthology.org/Q19-1016/)
  — the text conversational-QA source behind later spoken adaptations.
- [Pengi: An Audio Language Model for Audio Tasks](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/3a2e5889b4bbef997ddb13b55d5acf77-Abstract-Conference.html)
  — unified audio-language modeling including AQA evaluation.
- [Audiopedia: Audio QA with Knowledge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.20619)
  — audio-grounded QA involving external knowledge, multiple audio items, and
  retrieval; treat it as a newer research direction and audit data generation.

## Recommended reading order

Read 1 → 3 → 5 → 6 → 9 → 10 first. This sequence moves from the classic spoken
document setup to environmental audio, dialect/fairness, conversation, and
modern audio-language evaluation. Then read the method and data-generation
papers according to the research gap you choose.

## Rules for extending this list

- Prefer proceedings/publisher pages, DOI records, or official project pages.
- Record the exact version and publication status; do not call a preprint
  peer-reviewed unless it has a confirmed venue.
- Follow backward citations and papers that cite the work; do not rely only on
  search ranking.
- For every dataset, record audio origin, annotation origin, license, splits,
  speakers/languages, access status, and known limitations.
- A paper may use real audio but generated questions—or TTS audio with human
  questions. “Real data” must be assessed field by field, not as one label.
